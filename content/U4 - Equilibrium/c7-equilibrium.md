# C7 - Chemical Equilibrium

## C7.1 - Chemical Systems in Balance

### Reversible Reaction and Chemical Equilibrium

- **reversible reaction:** chemical reaction that proceeds in both forward and reverse directions

*Example reaction, ammonia prod:*

$$
\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) <=> 2NH3(g)}
$$

- Suppose nitrogen and hydrogen are put together
- Init concentration of reactants: high
- Init concentration of ammonia: 0
- As reaction proceeds, amnt. of reactant decreases
	- amnt. of ammonia increases
- System eventually reaches *equilibrium*
- **chemical equilibrium:** state in chemical system where the forward and reverse reaction are occuring at the same rate

*Equilibrium concentrations chart:*

![Chart](img/c7/c7.1-equilibrium-c-chart.png)

#### Dynamic Equilibrium

- in chemical equilibrium, reactions happen in both directions but at same rate
- no net change in products or reactants
- however, reactions in both directions continue to occur
- ammonia prod. in equilibrium has *macroscopic property* of constant concentrations
- **macroscopic property:** observable / measurable property
	- i.e. concentration, colour, temp. pressure, pH
- **dynamic equilibrium:** chemical system at equilibrium which is changing at molecular level
	- while macroscopic properties remain constant
- equilibrium does not mean concentrations have to be the same

#### Writing Equilibrium Equations

- double arrow ($\ce{<=>}$) indicates reversibility
- L to R: forward reaction
- R to L: reverse reaction

*Example*

$$
\ce{N2 + 3H2 <=> 2NH3}
$$

### Equilibrium Involving Physical Systems

- some equilibrium systems involve one substance in two physical states
- consider: water evaporating away from a flask
	- at surface of water, some water molecules have enough kinetic energy
	- and are moving in right direction to overcome IMFs of other water mcls.
	- these fast-moving water mcls. vaporize and escape into air
	- nearby water mcls. fill in the gap left, and process repeats
- however, you plug the flask
	- some water mcls. at surface escape and vaporize
	- as more water mcls. vaporize, pressure of vapor above liquid increases
	- some vapor mcls. collide w/ molecules at surface of liquid
	- if KE of vapor mcls. too low, they can't escape IMFs of liquid and thus condense
	- eventually, rate of condensation = rate of evaporation
	- *dynamic equilibrium* (amounts are constant)

![Illustration](img/c7/c7.1-phys-equilibrium-diag.png)

*Graph of vapour pressure v. time*

![Graph](img/c7/c7.1-phys-equilibrium-graph.png)

*2nd example:*

- iodine in closed flask
- some iodine sublime (s to g)
- gaseous iodine w/ lower KE deposit (g to s)
- *dynamic equilibrium*

### Conditions Necessary to Establish Equilbirium

- System must be *closed*
	- i.e. in water vapour, the gaseous mcls. could escape when system was open
	- no other mcls. / surrounding environ. may interfere w/ system

#### Thermal Energy Requirements of Reversible Reactions

- in reversible reaction
	- reaction in one direction: exothermic
	- reaction in other direction: endothermic
	- vice versa
- changing temp. alters rate of fwd. and rev. reactions
- temp. inc. adds thermal energy
	- causes reaction that absorbs thermal energy to proceed faster
	- than reaction that releases thermal energy
- temp. inc. rem. thermal energy
	- favours reaction that releases thermal energy

### Homogeneous and Heterogeneous Equilibria

- **homogeneous equilibrium:** chemical sys. in equilibrium where all components have the same physical state
- **heterogeneous equilibrium:** chemical sys. in equilibrium where components have diff. physical states

*Homogeneous equilibrium ex:*

$$
\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) <=> 2NH3(g)}
$$

Prod. of ammonia

*Heterogeneous equilibrium ex:*

$$
\ce{H2O(l) <=> H2O(g)}
$$

Water changing state

$$
\ce{CaCO3(s) <=> CaO(s) + CO2(g)}
$$

limestone decomposes to form quicklime and carbon dioxide

### Common Factors in Equilibrium Systems

- system in *equilibrium* when rate of forward process = rate of reverse process
- *macroscopic properties* are *constant* in equilibrium
- equilibrium can only be reached in closed system
	- where matter cannot cross sys. boundary
	- true equilibrium occurs in isolated systems
- equilibrium can be established from either direction

### Equilibrium Constant Expression

#### Law of Chemical Equilibrium

1864: Norwegian chemists Cato Guldberg and Peter Waage proposed *law of chemical equilibrium*

> **Law of Chemical Equilibrium** a.k.a. *Law of Mass Action*
>
> In a chemical system at equilibrium, there is a constant ratio between the concentrations of the products and the concentrations of the reactants.

#### Understanding the Law of Chemical Equilibrium

Example reaction:

$$
\ce{N2O4(g) <=> 2NO2(g)}
$$

dinitrogen oxide (colourless) converts into nitrogen dioxide (brown)

- small qty. of dinitrogen oxide added into bottle
- initial rate of forward reaction is very fast
- initial rate of rev. reaction is 0
- rate fwd. dec. while rate rev. inc.
- at equilibrium, both reach same rate
- there is a constant ratio between product and reactant ratios at equilibrium

![Reaction visual](img/c7/c7.1-N2O4-to-NO2.png)

#### Deriving Equilibrium Constant, $K_\text{eq}$

*Using example reaction above*

At equilibrium, rate of fwd. reaction = rate of rev. react.

$$
r_\text{fwd} = r_\text{rev}
$$

- both reactions are elementary steps
- so rate laws can be written from balanced chem. equation
- "eq" denotes equilibrium

$$
k_\text{fwd}[\ce{N2O4}]_\text{eq} = k_\text{rev}[\ce{NO2}]^2_\text{eq}
$$

Writing rate law equations as ratio

$$
\frac{k_\text{fwd}}{k_\text{rev}} = \frac{[\ce{NO2}]^2_\text{eq}}{[\ce{N2O4}]_\text{eq}}
$$

Ratio is rate law equations is called equilibrium constant, $K_\text{eq}$

$$
K_\text{eq} = \frac{[\ce{NO2}]^2_\text{eq}}{[\ce{N2O4}]_\text{eq}}
$$

- **equilibrium constant ($K_\text{eq}$):** ratio of equilibrium concentrations at particular temp. for particular chem. sys.
	- no units
	- each conc. value div. by reference state, and has no units

*General equilibrium reaction:*

$$
\ce{aA + bB <=> cC + dD}
$$

**Equilibrium Constant Expression:**

$$
\therefore K_\text{eq} = \frac{[\text C]^c[\text D]^d}{[\text A]^a[\text B]^b} = \frac{[\text{products}]}{[\text{reactants}]}
$$

- $[\text A], [\text B]$ &mdash; concentrations of reactants at equilibrium
- $[\text C], [\text D]$ &mdash; concentrations of products at equilibrium
- $a, b, c, d$ &mdash; stochiometric coefficients of each component in equation

*Position of Equilibrium:*

- When $K_\text{eq} > 1$, product formation is favoured
	- *equilibrium lies to the right*
	- $K_\text{eq} > 10^{10}$ indicates reaction will proceed to completion
- When $K_\text{eq} \approx 1$, system in equilibrium
	- approx. equal amounts of reactants and products at equilibrium
- When $K_\text{eq} < 1$, reactant formation is favoured
	- *equilibrium lies to the left*
	- $K_\text{eq} < 10^{-10}$ indicates reaction will not take place at all

![Diagram](img/c7/c7.1-K_eq-pr-form.png)

#### Ex: Expressions for Homogeneous Equilibrium

Simply use balanced chemical equation

> **Problem:** Sulfuric acid is produced through the contact process, which involves the catalytic oxidation of sulfur dioxide as an intermediate reaction:
> 
> $\ce{2SO2(g) + O2(g) <=> 2SO3(g)}$
>
> Write equilibrium expression for this process.

**Required:** Expression for $K_\text{eq}$

**Given:** Balanced chem. eq.

**Solution:**

*Utilize chemical equation above to write equilibrium constant expression. Coefficient of 2 indicates squaring:*

$$
\therefore K_\text{eq} = \frac{[\ce{SO3}]^2}{[\ce{SO2}]^2[\ce{O2}]}
$$

**Check Solution:**

- Square brackets = concentration
- Products in num., reactants in den.

#### Expressions for Heterogeneous Equilibrium

*Example:* Dynamic liquid-vapour equilibrium of ethanol

$$
\ce{C2H5OH(l) <=> C2H5OH(g)}
$$

Writing equilibrium expression for above using general form:

$$
K = \frac{[\ce{C2H5OH(g)}]}{[\ce{C2H5OH(l)}]}
$$

Liquid ethanol has constant concentration, so it is brought outside expression:

$$
[\ce{C2H5OH(l)}]K = [\ce{C2H5OH(g)}]
$$

Conc. of pure substance ethanol is combined w/ $K$, and that becomes equilibrium constant

$$
K_\text{eq} = [\ce{C2H5OH(g)}]
$$

*Equilibrium constant depends only on concentration of gaseous ethanol*

#### Pure Substances in Equilibrium Expressions

- Pure liquids and solids are not included in equilibrium constant expressions
	- Reason: Their concentrations remain constant
- Only gases and solutions are included in expression

## C7.2 - Effects of External Changes on Equilibrium

### Le Châtelier's Principle

Proposed by Henri Le Châtelier, French chemist in 1888

> **Le Châtelier's Principle:**
> 
> If an external stress (a change in concentration, pressure, volume, or temperature) is applied to a chemical system at equilibrium, ...
> 
> the rates of the forward and reverse reactions are temporarily unequal because the stress affects the reaction rates.
> 
> However, equilibrium is eventually restored in the chemical system.

#### Effects of Concentration Changes on Equilibrium

*Example:* Haber-Bosch process to manufacture ammonia

$$
\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) <=> 2NH3(g)}
$$

- ammonia removed from mixture of gases by cooling
- when temp. decreases, ammonia liquifies before nitrogen or hydrogen
- when ammonia removed, equilibrium temporarily shifts to right
	- fewer ammonia to do rev. reaction
- more ammonia is prod. until equilibrium is reached
- if more hydrogen and nitrogen added, more mcls. to do fwd. reaction
	- more ammonia prod.

*Reaction vessel for ammonia:*

![Diagram](img/c7/c7.2-ammonia.png)

*Example:* Dinitrogen tetroxide to nitrogen dioxide

$$
\ce{N2O4(g) <=> 2NO2(g)}
$$

- when more dinitrogen tetroxide is injected, that gas has a sharp increase
- concentration then immediately begins to drop
- rate of rev. reaction also begins to increase
- *KEY:* ratio of new concentrations of products to reactants is SAME as original ratio

*Graph of concentration v. time for reaction above:*

![Graph](img/c7/c7.2-N2O4-graph.png)

**Summary of Concentration Effects:**

- Increasing conc. of product causes reaction to shift left (reactants)
- Decreasing conc. of product causes reaction to shift right (products)
- Increasing conc. of reactant causes reaction to shift right (products)
- Decreasing conc. of reactant causes reaction to shift left (reactants)
- Equilibrium tries to "fill" the "missing" components
- $K_\text{eq}$ unaffected, since product : reactant ratio remains constant

*Concentration changes in body:*

Carbonic acid in blood:

$$
\ce{H2CO3(aq) <=> CO2(aq) + H2O(l)}
$$

- body controls levels of carbonic acid by rem. carbon dioxide
- decreases carbonic acid in blood
- helps maintain safe levels of this acid

#### Effects of Volume and Pressure Changes on Equilibrium

- when vol. of gas mixture decreases, pressure of gases increases
- **Boyle's law** states volume of fixed amount of gas at constant temp. varies inversely w/ pressure
	- equation: $V \propto \dfrac 1 P$

*Example:* Decomp. of sulfur trioxide

$$
\ce{2SO3(g) <=> 2SO2(g) + O2(g)}
$$

- when vol. of reaction vessel suddenly decreases (P inc.), system no longer at equilibrium
- volume change &rarr; concentration change
- increase in reactant conc. causes inc. in mcl. collisions
- inc. in fwd. and rev. rate
- gas pressure caused by gas particles striking walls of reaction vessel
- to restore equilibrium, reaction temporarily shifts in dir. that decreases no. of molecules and at same time, decreases system pressure
- to red. pressure here, rev. reaction favoured bcz. less gas mcls.
- if pressure decreases, reaction temp. shifts in dir. w/ more gas mcls.
- ratio of product concentrations to reactant conc. does not change

*Concentration v. time graph*

![Graph](img/c7/c7.2-PV-ch-cvt-graph.png)

Notice the sudden spike of all components

*Illustration of equilibrium shift*

![Illustration](img/c7/c7.2-gas-eq-shift.png)

- if reaction has same no. of gas mcls. on both sides
	- inc. of fwd. rate = inc. in rev. rate
	- no effect on equilibrium
- inert gas can also be used to increase pressure
	- does not react w/ system components
	- higher component conc.
	- equal conc. changes does not cause shift

**Summary of effects of pressure-volume changes:**

- If pressure increases, reaction shifts so that total gas particles decreases (less gas)
	- or volume decrease
	- dec. pressure
- If pressure decreases, reaction shifts so that total gas particles increases (more gas)
	- or volume increase
	- inc. pressure
- Inert gas added to system at equilibrium does not cause shift
- $K_\text{eq}$ unaffected because products : reactants is constant

*Example: Freeze-drying foods*

1. Freeze foods so that water becomes ice
2. Pressurize container in which food is in
	1. When pressure decreases, water in food sublimes
3. Remove water vapour from vessel

$$
\ce{H2O(s) <=> H2O(g)}
$$

#### Effects of Temperature Changes on Equilibrium

*Example:* Decomposition of dinitrogen tetroxide, endothermic

$$
\ce{N2O4(g) + 58.0 kJ/mol <=> 2NO2(g)}
$$

- if chemical system cooled, energy in system decreases
- less energy available for fwd. reaction
	- net result: rev. reaction occurs faster than fwd. reaction
	- reaction shifts to left
- as reaction shifts to left, amount of energy in system increases too
- as energy increases, fwd. reaction rate inc.
- eventually, equilibrium reached
- when temp. increases (L to R), endothermic reaction is favoured
	- $K_\text{eq}$ increases as a result
- when temp. decreases (R to L), exothermic reaction is favoured
	- $K_\text{eq}$ decreases as a result

**Summary of Temperature Changes:**

- Temp. increase favours endothermic reaction
	- Shifts away from heat if it were a prod. / react.
- Temp. decrease favours exothermic reaction
	- Shifts toward heat
- In endothermic sys., temp. inc. increases $K_\text{eq}$
	- and vice versa
- In exothermic sys., temp. inc. decreases $K_\text{eq}$
	- and vice versa

*Concentration v. time graphs for endothermic v. exothermic reactions, on temp. increase:*

![Graphs](img/c7/c7.2-exo-v-endo-T-inc.png)

#### Effect of Catalyst on Equilibrium

- Catalyst increases rates of reaction in both directions, when added
- System reaches equilibrium, but catalyst does not shift equilibrium
- $K_\text{eq}$ remains unaffected

> Presence of catalyst does not shift equilibrium.

### The Equilibrium Constant, $K_p$

- chem. system w/ only gases can have equilibrium expressed using gas partial pressures
	- for gases, easier to measure partial pressure
- ideal gas law can be used to derive equilibrium constant, as long as all gases are ideal

Ideal gas law, rearranged for concentration

$$
\begin{align*}
PV &= nRT \\
\frac P{RT} &= \frac n V = c
\end{align*}
$$

- $P$ &mdash; pressure (kPa)
- $R$ &mdash; universal gas constant
- $n$ &mdash; amount of gas (mol)
- $V$ &mdash; volume (L)

#### Calculating $K_p$

*Example:*

$$
\ce{CO(g) + 3H2(g) <=> CH4(g) + H2O(g)}
$$

Molar concentrations are replaced with partial pressures

$$
K_p = \frac{P_\ce{CH4}P_\ce{H2O}}{P_\ce{CO}(P_\ce{H2})^3}
$$

Generic equation, letters have same meaning:

$$
\begin{gather*}
\ce{aA + bB <=> cC + dD} \\
K_p = \frac{(P_\text C)^c(P_\text D)^d}{P_\text A)^a(P_\text B)^b}
\end{gather*}
$$

Values of $K_\text{eq}$ and $K_p$ are **NOT** the same

Convert $K_p$ to $K_\text{eq}$

$$
K_p = K_\text{eq}(RT)^{\Delta n}
$$

$\Delta n$ = sum of coeff. of gas products - sum of coeff. of gas reactants

### Reaction Quotients, $Q_\text{eq}$ and $Q_p$

- **reaction quotient ($Q$):** numerical value determined by using same formula as equilibrium constant,
	- using data for reversible reaction that may / may not be at equilibrium

*General reaction formula:*

$$
\ce{aA + bB <=> cC + dD}
$$

*Reaction quotient formulas:*

$$
\begin{gather*}
Q_\text{eq} = \frac{[\text C]^c[\text D]^d}{[\text A]^a[\text B]^b} \\
Q_p = \frac{(P_\text C)^c(P_\text D)^d}{(P_\text A)^a(P_\text B)^b}
\end{gather*}
$$

*Q* can be used to predict reaction behaviour

Rules below apply to both "eq" and "p" variants:

- P : R = ratio of products to reactants
- If $Q < K$, then $P:R < K$ &mdash; *Reaction shifts to right*
	- More products need to form and reactants need to be consumed to reach equilibrium
- If $Q = K$, then system at equilibrium
- If $Q > K$, then $P:R > K$ &mdash; *Reaction shifts to left*
	- Products must decompose into reactants to reach equilibrium
